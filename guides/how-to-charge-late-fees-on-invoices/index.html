How to charge late fees on invoices

Late fees only work if your contract allows them — and even then, the bigger hit is often opportunity cost while cash sits unpaid.

Not legal advice. US rules vary by state and contract type. This is a general freelancer overview so you can estimate numbers, not draft counsel.

1. Put it in the agreement first

Courts and clients both care about notice. Spell out: when fees start (after net-15/net-30 + grace), whether it’s a % per month or flat fee, and how invoices show the charge.

2. Pick a fee you can actually collect

Aggressive fees look tough on paper and often never get paid. Many freelancers use ~1–1.5%/month or a modest flat fee. Run both in CashGap before you rewrite your MSA.

3. Separate “contract fee” from “cash gap”

Even with $0 collectible fees, money stuck in AR isn’t earning (or paying your bills). Opportunity drag is why late clients feel expensive beyond the fee line item.

4. Remind before you escalate

A calm reminder sequence recovers more cash than a surprise fee. Pair process with tools that auto-nudge (Wave, Melio).
